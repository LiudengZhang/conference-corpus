1 st part:
For the presentation, I would start with a conceptual map of the dream that our computational biologist want to do: we want to reproduce a real cell in physical world in silico. And that means many aspect: we wnat to know how woold a typical cell looks like (descriptive); and we also want to know, across different cells, which gene would correlate with the other and in what form they would correlate (correlation/gene program); how it would behave once we give it a singal, like a drug or cytokine (interreption); and we also want to know, how would a cell be after 10 days under a certain situation (prediction); and eventually, we want to know, that I know that the cell have been in a certain situation, but I want to change it destine to another state, what should I do (counterfactual). So that is the larger picture and there are different ladders.
2 nd part:
So with this ambition, people start to build virtual cells, and in some early attempts they treat cell more like a machine and they want to know how does each items in this machine looks like (programs) and assemble them together as a predictive model. Such track is failed, and for a long time people didn't try to make a vurtual cell. However, with transformers and single cell atlas / datasets, people started attempts again, and then we have several papers like scBERT, Geneformer, scGPT and scFoundation, etc. We would name thsoe as 'Early Foundation Models', they have certain features: they are all based on transformer and single cell atlas (like CELLxGENE), they mask cell types for prediction, but those early foundation models eventually was beat by linear model. And people need to know why.
3rd part:
So people digging into this question, and found several hints, like such training process would not really do a good prediction of perturbation (so where are they in our 5 step ladder?), etc. And people started to make new atlas, which are based on perturb-seq, and then people get new datasets like Tahoe 100M and new foundation models like STATE and Tahoe-x1. That is a new era of virtual cell which eventually focus on perturbation. In this era, benchmarks report that foundation models, while a small portion of them fail but those bigger and newer one do success when competiting with linear model. ANd also, some groups are intetrested in how to understand thsoe foundation model and build tools like SAE for a deeper understanding.
4th part:
However there are still limitations in prediction. And people gradually realize, that it is not only the question of scale, with smaller but fine tuned model with good context win from the competition with the gaint fundatuon models. It is a time for algorithm develop, so we meet the 2-direction era: while the large company and institute continued to build those foudnation model, smaller groups, especially thsoe computational scientists and statisticians start to innovate from the modeling view.
5th part:
One of the trend, is to use distribution based method, like diffusion, flow matching and diffusion transformer, all 3 are tools originally used in CV but transplanted into biological field. And as biological data is hudge and sparse, latent diffusion was used. And in that aspect, a group of tools were born, like CFGen, scDiffusion, CellFlow, scDiffEq, TranscripFomer, etc. And some other tools, which might not be strictly count as virtual cell relevant tool or perturbation predictor, like RegVelo, is also contributing to this domain and worthy for attention.
6th part:
But that is still not casuality. People are starting to focus more on the casual discovery from interventions, which to be honest, is still pretty young field in VC. Tools like Waddington-OT, CellOT and moscot are thsoe leading works, which can be rooted from older tools like Mixcap and GEARs. That should be a future direction, and a casual map might be created and be the most expectable tool in future. And at the mean time, a further explaination of the virtual cell model, would also be in need (current SAE based method is still too naive). And for pratical usage, a prediction on the single cell / single patient / single cohort level is super important, which we haven't reach yet. 
7th part:
So in the end, we should have something like a summary. So what is the era of now, when we have powerful models like STATE, large atlas like Tahoe 100M, standard pipeline like Pertpy? And in future, we should expect that a revolution on computational biology is approaching.
