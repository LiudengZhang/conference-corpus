# PSB 2026

**Pacific Symposium on Biocomputing 2026** — Fairmont Orchid, Kohala Coast, Big Island of Hawaii · **January 3–7, 2026** (workshops Jan 3; scientific program Jan 4–7).

> **Status: ✅ built from the open proceedings.** Unlike most of the corpus, this vault is not a scaffold and not transcript-derived — PSB publishes **full peer-reviewed papers, free, before the meeting**. All 55 papers, 5 session introductions, and 5 workshop introductions are open PDFs with PubMed records. Everything here is sourced from `psb.stanford.edu`; per-paper detail lives on the session pages.

## Why this is in the vault

**PSB is the highest-yield harvest in the corpus, per unit of effort.** Every other concluded meeting we track gives us abstracts, slides, or auto-generated captions. PSB gives complete, peer-reviewed, openly licensed papers — three referees each — indexed in PubMed within weeks. There is no embargo to wait out and no paywall to route around.

Three further reasons it earns a vault:

1. **Foundation models and LLMs are the dominant theme.** At least 20 of 55 papers are LLM- or FM-centric, and unusually many of them are *evaluations* rather than model announcements — including a multi-institution negative result ([Automated Chest X-ray Report Generation Remains Unsolved](sessions/ai-ml-clinical-medicine.md)). That is exactly the critical-evaluation posture this corpus takes in [Why linear baselines win](../../talks/fm-to-virtual-cells/why-linear-baselines-win.md) and the [evaluation papers catalog](../../talks/fm-to-virtual-cells/evaluation-papers-catalog.md).
2. **It is the front edge of LLM agents in biology.** Four papers apply agentic LLMs to biological tasks — gene-set analysis, protein function prediction, disease-relationship discovery, and clinical agent benchmarking — which is the corpus's [agentic-ai](../aacr-2026/topics/agentic-ai/index.md) thread with published methods attached.
3. **PSB's session model makes it a leading indicator.** Sessions are proposed and selected fresh each year rather than fixed, so the track list is itself a signal about which subfields the community thinks are emerging. See [Themes](themes.md).

**What PSB is not:** there is **no cancer session**. Oncology enters through methods papers — see the [cancer thread](themes.md#cancer-and-oncology) for the four that matter.

## Program shape

PSB's format is unusual and worth knowing before reading the session pages. Each peer-reviewed session runs: a 10-minute organizer introduction (published as a paper), ~15-minute talks per accepted paper, then **a full hour of open discussion**. Around that sit five non-peer-reviewed workshops, invited "TERI Talks," a poster session, two keynotes, and the Research Parasite / Research Symbiont Awards.

## Known data caveats

- **License is CC BY-NC 4.0, not CC BY.** The proceedings TOC page claims CC BY 4.0, but the copyright line inside each paper and the PubMed record both say **non-commercial**. Treat as CC BY-NC.
- **One paper is unlinked from the TOC.** `cardone.pdf` (Cardone et al., pp. 629–643) exists and returns HTTP 200 but has no link on the proceedings index — a link scrape will silently miss it. Direct URL: <https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/cardone.pdf>
- **One page range conflicts.** `rifat.pdf` is listed as pp. 644–665 on the TOC but 644–663 in PubMed; the next paper starts at 664, so PubMed is likely right.
- **A name is rendered two ways.** The Fairness session organizer appears as "Madalena Ng" on the TOC and preface, "Magdalena Ng" in the session-intro byline.
- **No acceptance rate is published.** None appears in the preface, post-event site, or schedule. Third-party sites quote a generic figure for PSB; do not cite it as a 2026 fact.
- **World Scientific blocks automated fetching** (HTTP 403). ISBN and DOI facts here are cross-checked from the master-PDF filename, the chapter DOI prefix, and retailer listings rather than read off the publisher's copyright page.
