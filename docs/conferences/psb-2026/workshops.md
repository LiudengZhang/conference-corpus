# PSB 2026 — Workshops

Five workshops ran alongside the peer-reviewed program. They are **not peer-reviewed** — invited-speaker format — but each publishes a short introduction in the proceedings (pp. 849–868), so the topics and organizers are citable.

!!! note "The site undercounts these"
    The PSB post-event page and workshop page both say "four workshops." The preface, the proceedings table of contents, and the schedule all show **five**, and five introductions are published. Five is correct.

## 1. Advances of AI Methods in Single Cell Spatial Omics

**Organizers:** Lana Garmire · Xiuwei Zhang (Georgia Tech) · Joshua Levy
**Slot:** Saturday, January 3, 1:00–4:45 p.m., Salon 2 & 3
**Introduction:** pp. 849–851 · [workshop_advances.pdf](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/workshop_advances.pdf) · PMID 41758189
**Site:** <https://sites.google.com/view/ai-spatial-omics-psb2026>

Spatial transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. **This is where PSB 2026's single-cell and spatial content actually lives** — the peer-reviewed sessions contain almost none (see [Themes](themes.md#single-cell-and-spatial-omics)). For this corpus, that makes the workshop introduction and its agenda the highest-value item in this file.

## 2. AI for Health: Leveraging Artificial Intelligence to Revolutionize Healthcare

**Organizers:** Ruowang Li · Tiffani Bright · Brian D. Davison · Lifang He
**Introduction:** pp. 852–854 · [workshop_aiforhealth.pdf](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/workshop_aiforhealth.pdf) · PMID 41758190
**Site:** <https://ruowangli.github.io/psb_workshop_2026>

The clinical-AI companion to [session S4](sessions/ai-ml-clinical-medicine.md).

## 3. Applications of AI & ML in Biomanufacturing of Cell and Gene Therapies

**Organizers:** Eric Neumann · Karen Weisinger · Tom Londo
**Introduction:** pp. 855–858 · [workshop_appaiml.pdf](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/workshop_appaiml.pdf) · PMID 41758191

Manufacturing rather than discovery — the part of the cell-and-gene-therapy pipeline the corpus's trial-focused vaults ([`sitc-2026/trials/`](../sitc-2026/trials/index.md)) do not cover. Pairs with the AAV2 capsid design paper in [S2](sessions/biological-molecular-function.md).

## 4. The Evolving Cyberinfrastructure at the NIH to Support Data and AI in Biomedical Research

**Organizers:** Ojas A. Ramwala · Nick Weber · Sean D. Mooney
**Introduction:** pp. 859–864 · [workshop_evolving.pdf](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/workshop_evolving.pdf) · PMID 41758192
**Site:** <https://weber-nick.github.io/psb-2026/>

Policy and platform infrastructure — worth tracking for anyone whose data access depends on NIH-hosted resources.

## 5. Trust, Reproducibility, and Progress: The Roles of Independent Blind Prediction and Assessment and Benchmarking in Computational Biology

**Organizers:** Gaia Andreoletti · Serghei Mangul · Mark Robinson · Predrag Radivojac · Steven E. Brenner
**Introduction:** pp. 865–868 · [workshop_trust.pdf](https://psb.stanford.edu/psb-online/proceedings/psb26/workshop_trust.pdf) · PMID 41758193

**The intellectual counterweight to the rest of the meeting.** Blind assessment (the CASP/CAFA tradition) as the answer to benchmark inflation. Read alongside the negative-result and benchmark papers in [S4](sessions/ai-ml-clinical-medicine.md), and against the corpus's own evaluation material in [Reading an FM paper critically](../../talks/fm-to-virtual-cells/reading-an-fm-paper-critically.md).
