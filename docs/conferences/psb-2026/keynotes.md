Two keynotes, four "TERI Talks" (invited short talks), four session invited talks, and an NSF agency talk. Slot times from the [official schedule PDF](https://psb.stanford.edu/previous/psb26/conference-materials/schedule.pdf); keynote abstracts and bios from the [keynote page](https://psb.stanford.edu/previous/psb26/keynote/).

## Keynote 1 — Science keynote

**Eric Green, MD, PhD** — former Director, National Human Genome Research Institute, NIH
**Title:** *"From the Human Genome Project to the Realization of Genomic Medicine: Highlights from a ~35-Year Journey"*
**Slot:** Sunday, January 4, 12:00–1:00 p.m., Salon 2 & 3 · introduced by Teri Klein

Green directed NHGRI from 2009 to 2025 and spent more than 30 years at the institute — seven as Scientific Director, thirteen as Chief of the Genome Technology Branch, and twelve as founding Director of the NIH Intramural Sequencing Center. MD/PhD from Washington University in St. Louis (1987); elected to the National Academy of Medicine in 2023.

A retrospective from the person who ran the US genomics program through its entire translational arc. For a corpus built around what genomics-derived models can and cannot yet do clinically, this is the institutional-memory counterweight to the meeting's foundation-model enthusiasm.

## Keynote 2 — Ethical, Legal and Social Implications keynote

**Luis Campos** — Baker College Chair for the History of Science, Technology, and Innovation, Rice University
**Title:** *"The Best that Biochemistry and Starshine Can Do: Chance, Necessity, and Urgency"*
**Slot:** Tuesday, January 6, 7:25–8:30 p.m., Coconut Grove (following the banquet) · introduced by Russ Altman

Campos co-organized "The Spirit of Asilomar and the Future of Biotechnology," marking the 50th anniversary of the Asilomar conference on recombinant DNA. He wrote *Radium and the Secret of Life* (2015) and held the 4th Baruch S. Blumberg/NASA Chair of Astrobiology at the Library of Congress (2016–17).

Placing a historian of Asilomar in the ELSI keynote slot, in a year when the meeting's dominant theme is AI agents doing biology, is a pointed piece of programming.
